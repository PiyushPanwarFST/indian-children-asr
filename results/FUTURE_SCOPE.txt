Future Scope: Improving Dual-Encoder Fusion for Indian Children's ASR
======================================================================

Current best result:
  Acoustic branch (single encoder):  19.96% WER
  Combined gated fusion:             18.92% WER  (-1.04% improvement)

The 1% improvement is modest because both encoders learn similar acoustic
representations (both distilled from speech models processing the same mel
input). Below are two concrete directions to improve this.


======================================================================
DIRECTION 1: Unfreezing Encoders During Fusion Training
======================================================================

STATUS: Implemented, ready to run.

WHAT:
  Currently both encoders are FROZEN during fusion training — only the
  gate network (~590K params) and CTC head (~65K params) train.

  Proposed change: UNFREEZE both encoders with a small learning rate
  (encoder_lr = 1e-5) while fusion + CTC head train at normal LR (1e-3).
  This 100x difference is called "differential learning rates."

WHY IT SHOULD HELP:
  When frozen, the encoders produce whatever features they learned
  independently. They were never trained to work TOGETHER. The fusion
  gate can only blend what it's given — it can't make the encoders
  produce better features.

  When unfrozen, the CTC loss gradient flows back through the fusion
  gate into BOTH encoders:

    CTC Loss
      -> gradient to CTC head (update weights)
      -> gradient to fusion gate (update weights)
      -> gradient through gate to acoustic encoder (small update)
      -> gradient through (1-gate) to semantic encoder (small update)

  Over time, if the acoustic encoder already captures a certain pattern
  well (gate -> 1.0 for that pattern), the semantic encoder gets near-zero
  gradient for that pattern and is pushed to learn SOMETHING ELSE.
  This naturally forces specialization and complementarity.

RISK - CATASTROPHIC FORGETTING:
  If encoder LR is too high, the encoder "forgets" its pretrained
  knowledge and WER degrades. Mitigation:
    - Very small encoder LR (1e-5 = 100x smaller than fusion LR)
    - Early stopping on dev WER
    - Gradient clipping (max_norm=1.0)

TECHNIQUE REFERENCE:
  - "Differential learning rates" — Howard & Ruder, 2018
    "Universal Language Model Fine-tuning for Text Classification" (ULMFiT)
    They showed that fine-tuning pretrained models with progressively
    smaller LRs in lower layers prevents catastrophic forgetting while
    allowing task-specific adaptation.

EXPECTED IMPACT: 1-3% additional WER reduction.


======================================================================
DIRECTION 2: Cross-Modal Knowledge Distillation (MuRIL Integration)
======================================================================

STATUS: Proposed. Requires new training script.

PROBLEM WITH CURRENT SEMANTIC BRANCH:
  The semantic encoder is distilled from IndicConformer — another SPEECH
  model. So it learns acoustic features, making it redundant with the
  acoustic encoder. Both encoders process the same mel spectrogram through
  the same Whisper architecture and learn overlapping representations.

  For gated fusion to work well, the two encoders must capture DIFFERENT
  information. Currently they don't.

PROPOSED SOLUTION:
  Replace IndicConformer (speech teacher) with MuRIL (text teacher) for
  the semantic branch. MuRIL is Google's Multilingual Representations for
  Indian Languages — a BERT-based model trained on text in 17 Indian
  languages including Hindi, Marathi, and English.

  This forces the semantic encoder to learn MEANING-level features
  (grammar, word relationships, semantics) instead of acoustic features.

WHY MuRIL (not IndicBERT):
  - MuRIL: BERT-base, 110M params, 768-dim output (matches Whisper Small)
    Trained on Wikipedia + CommonCrawl + translated parallel text
    17 Indian languages, strong cross-lingual transfer
    HuggingFace: google/muril-base-cased

  - IndicBERT: ALBERT, 18M params, 768-dim output
    Trained on IndicCorp (9B tokens), 12 Indian languages
    Weaker representations due to smaller model and data

  MuRIL is preferred because:
    1. Output dim 768 matches Whisper Small — no projection layer needed
    2. Trained on parallel translated text — better cross-lingual features
       (important: our children code-switch between Hindi/English/Marathi)
    3. Stronger base model (BERT-base vs ALBERT)


HOW CROSS-MODAL DISTILLATION WORKS:
------------------------------------

The key insight: during training, we HAVE the ground truth transcript
for every audio clip. We can pass the transcript through MuRIL to get
"what the text means" and train the speech encoder to produce similar
representations from the audio alone.

  TRAINING TIME (transcript available):

    Ground Truth Text: "what is the time"
         |
         v
    MuRIL (frozen) ------> text_embeddings (N_tokens x 768)
                                |
                           [Alignment Layer]   <-- matches sequence lengths
                                |
                                v
                           aligned_text_emb (T_frames x 768)
                                |
                           MSE Loss  <----------- MINIMIZE THIS
                                |
    Audio (mel spectrogram)     |
         |                      |
         v                      |
    Whisper Encoder (student) -> speech_embeddings (T_frames x 768)


  INFERENCE TIME (no transcript):

    Audio (mel spectrogram)
         |
         v
    Whisper Encoder -> speech_embeddings (T_frames x 768)

    The encoder has LEARNED to produce "semantic" features from audio
    alone, without needing the text. MuRIL is NOT used at inference.


THE ALIGNMENT CHALLENGE:
  Text and audio have different sequence lengths:
    - MuRIL tokenizes "what is the time" into 5-6 tokens
    - Whisper produces ~100-1500 frames from the audio

  They must be aligned before computing MSE. Three approaches:

  Approach A: Utterance-Level Pooling (simplest)
    text_vec = mean(text_embeddings, dim=0)     -> (768,)
    speech_vec = mean(speech_embeddings, dim=0)  -> (768,)
    Loss = MSE(speech_vec, text_vec)

    Pros: Simple, no alignment needed
    Cons: Loses all temporal/positional information

  Approach B: CTC-Based Forced Alignment (most principled)
    Use CTC alignment to find which audio frames correspond to which
    characters/tokens. Average frame embeddings per token position.

    Example: "w-h-a-t" aligned to frames [10-15, 16-20, 21-25, 26-30]
    text_token_1 = MuRIL("what")
    speech_token_1 = mean(speech_frames[10:30])
    Loss = MSE(speech_token_1, text_token_1)

    Pros: Linguistically principled, frame-level alignment
    Cons: Requires pre-trained CTC model for alignment (we have this)

  Approach C: Learned Projection (most flexible)
    Add a small cross-attention or linear projection layer that maps
    T_frames -> N_tokens or N_tokens -> T_frames.

    projection = Linear(T_frames, N_tokens)  or  CrossAttention
    aligned_text = projection(text_embeddings)  -> (T_frames x 768)
    Loss = MSE(speech_embeddings, aligned_text)

    Pros: End-to-end learnable, no external alignment
    Cons: More parameters, needs careful training

  RECOMMENDATION: Start with Approach A (utterance-level pooling) as
  baseline — fast to implement, gives immediate signal. Then try
  Approach B with our existing CTC model for frame-level alignment.


TRAINING PIPELINE WITH MuRIL:
  Stage 1: (already done) Train acoustic encoder via Kid-Whisper KD
  Stage 2: (NEW) Train semantic encoder via MuRIL cross-modal KD
            Loss = MSE(whisper_encoder_output, muril_aligned_output)
  Stage 3: (same) Gated fusion of acoustic + semantic encoders

  The only change is Stage 2's teacher: IndicConformer -> MuRIL.
  Everything else (fusion architecture, CTC head, training procedure)
  stays the same.


WHAT THE SEMANTIC ENCODER LEARNS (with MuRIL vs IndicConformer):

  With IndicConformer teacher (current):
    - Spectral patterns of Indian phonemes
    - Pronunciation models from 17K hours of adult Indian speech
    - Acoustic features (redundant with acoustic branch)

  With MuRIL teacher (proposed):
    - Word-level semantic relationships ("ghar" and "house" are related)
    - Language-specific grammar patterns (Hindi SOV vs English SVO)
    - Cross-lingual mappings (same meaning in different languages)
    - Contextual meaning (same word sounds different in different contexts)

  The acoustic branch knows HOW words SOUND.
  The MuRIL-trained semantic branch knows WHAT words MEAN.
  These are genuinely complementary — exactly what gated fusion needs.


TECHNIQUE REFERENCES:
  Cross-modal knowledge distillation (speech-text):
    - Bapna et al., 2021: "SLAM: A Unified Encoder for Speech and
      Language Modeling via Speech-Text Joint Pre-Training" (Google)
      Key idea: align speech and text representations in shared space

    - Ao et al., 2022: "SpeechT5: Unified-Modal Encoder-Decoder
      Pre-Training for Spoken Language Processing" (Microsoft)
      Key idea: shared encoder processes both speech and text

    - Chen et al., 2022: "MAESTRO: Matched Speech Text Representations
      through Modality Matching" (Google)
      Key idea: use text encoder to guide speech encoder training

  Knowledge distillation (foundational):
    - Hinton et al., 2015: "Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network"
      The original KD paper — teacher-student framework

  MuRIL:
    - Khanuja et al., 2021: "MuRIL: Multilingual Representations for
      Indian Languages" (Google Research India)
      Pretrained on 17 Indian languages, 768-dim BERT-base

  Differential learning rates:
    - Howard & Ruder, 2018: "Universal Language Model Fine-tuning
      for Text Classification" (ULMFiT)


EXPECTED IMPACT:
  With MuRIL-based semantic encoder + unfrozen fusion:
    - Semantic branch standalone: likely still 40-50% WER (MSE from
      text model is harder to learn), BUT the features are now
      genuinely different from acoustic branch
    - Combined fusion: expected 3-5% improvement over acoustic-only
      (vs current 1%), potentially reaching 15-17% test WER

  This would be a significant result for children's ASR on Indian
  languages and a strong contribution to the thesis.
